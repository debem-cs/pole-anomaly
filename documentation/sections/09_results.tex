\chapter{v2: synthetic results}
\label{chap:results}

This chapter mirrors \cref{chap:v1results} on the v2 dataset. We look
at the training dynamics (\cref{sec:res-train}), the sample-level
accuracy (\cref{sec:res-window}), and the event-level metrics on a
held-out segment (\cref{sec:res-event}). The chapter closes with a
cost/performance comparison (\cref{sec:res-cost}).

\section{Training dynamics}
\label{sec:res-train}

\Cref{fig:v2-training} overlays the train and validation losses and
the validation macro F1 of the three models trained on the v2 dataset.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/v2_training_curves}
    \caption{v2 training dynamics. Left: train (dashed) and validation
    (solid) loss. Right: validation macro F1 (window level).}
    \label{fig:v2-training}
\end{figure}

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*, itemsep=0.25em]
    \item All three models converge well within $35$ epochs. The
    CNN+Attention model hits the early-stopping criterion first (epoch
    $21$), the ResNet shortly after (epoch $26$), and the 1D-CNN runs
    the full $35$ epochs.
    \item Validation losses are lower than in v1: the ResNet reaches
    $\sim 0.12$ (vs $\sim 0.57$ in v1), the CNN+Attention $\sim 0.13$
    (vs $\sim 0.62$), and the 1D-CNN $\sim 0.14$ (vs $\sim 0.73$).
    This is mostly a consequence of the simpler classification task
    --- four classes against seven, with the majority \texttt{Normal}
    class being trivial to separate from a bump.
    \item The validation F1 saturates around $0.89$--$0.92$ across the
    three models. The remaining gap is mostly window-level boundary
    fuzziness: under the event-coverage rule of \cref{sec:framing},
    the windows that capture between $\sim 25\%$ and $\sim 35\%$ of a
    short event sit on either side of the $\tau_{\text{cov}} = 0.30$
    threshold and flip easily.
\end{itemize}

\section{Event-level results}
\label{sec:res-event}

The window-level numbers in \cref{tab:test-window} are misleading at
the operational level for the same reason as in v1: a window inherits
the class of the event it captures, and the boundary windows of an
event flip between the anomaly class and \texttt{Normal} easily. The
right metric is event-level (\cref{sec:event-eval}), computed on a
continuous $100\,000$-sample segment.

On that segment the v2 generator places only $15$ ground-truth events
(against $59$ in the v1 dataset, an immediate consequence of the
density change). The event-level confusion matrices for the three
models are shown in \cref{fig:v2-confmat}.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/v2_confusion_matrices}
    \caption{v2 event-level confusion matrices on the synthetic test
    segment. The leftmost column of each panel is the ``missed event''
    column --- a true event that has no overlapping prediction. No
    event is missed by any of the three models, including the four
    shortest ones in the segment ($26$, $28$, $30$ and $45$ samples,
    near the lower bound of the v2 duration range). The 1D-CNN and
    CNN+Attention classify all $15$ events correctly; the ResNet has a
    single off-diagonal entry --- one $61$-sample \texttt{bell}
    predicted as \texttt{fast\_ascend}.}
    \label{fig:v2-confmat}
\end{figure}

The per-class precision/recall/F1 are summarised in
\cref{tab:v2-event-prf}.

\begin{table}[H]
    \centering
    \footnotesize
    \setlength{\tabcolsep}{4pt}
    \begin{tabular}{l ccc ccc ccc}
        \toprule
        & \multicolumn{3}{c}{\textbf{1D-CNN}} & \multicolumn{3}{c}{\textbf{ResNet 1D}} & \multicolumn{3}{c}{\textbf{CNN+Attention}} \\

    \caption{v2 per-class precision (P), recall (R) and F1 at the event

    long).}
    \label{tab:v2-event-prf}
\end{table}

\paragraph{The short events are all detected.} The four shortest
events of the segment ($26$, $28$, $30$ and $45$ samples) sit near
the lower bound of the v2 duration range, well below the $W = 512$
window length. They are correctly classified by all three
architectures, which confirms operationally that the event-coverage
labelling rule of \cref{sec:framing} keeps such events in the
training distribution: the network is given a positive example for
each one, and learns to fire on them at inference time.

\paragraph{The single residual error is on the ResNet.} The one
off-diagonal entry of \cref{tab:v2-event-prf} is a $61$-sample
\texttt{bell} that the ResNet predicts as \texttt{fast\_ascend} at
$78\%$ confidence (event $\#9$ in
\texttt{architectures/logs/resnet/inference\_log.txt}). It is a
short, asymmetric \texttt{bell} whose rising flank dominates the
window, which is genuinely \texttt{fast\_ascend}-like; the 1D-CNN and
CNN+Attention models classify it correctly. We do not consider this a
structural failure of the ResNet --- it is one event out of fifteen
on a single $100\,000$-sample test segment --- but flag it as a
genuine point of differentiation between the three architectures.

\paragraph{Precision and false positives.} No model produces a single
false positive on the segment ($P = 1.00$ for $8$ of the $9$
class--model combinations; the one $P < 1$ entry is a downstream
consequence of the single ResNet misclassification, not of an
extraneous event). The v2 networks do not hallucinate on this
segment.

\section{Cost--performance comparison}
\label{sec:res-cost}

\Cref{fig:model-cost} summarises the cost/performance trade-off across
the three architectures on the v2 setup.

\begin{figure}[H]
    \centering
    \includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{figures/model_cost}
    \caption{v2 cost--performance trade-off. \textbf{Left}: parameter
    count ($22.6$k, $74.5$k and $109.5$k for the 1D-CNN, ResNet and
    CNN+Attention respectively). \textbf{Centre}: total training time
    on a single RTX 5060 Laptop GPU (the 1D-CNN ran the full $35$
    epochs in $\sim 3.7$ min, the CNN+Attention stopped early at
    epoch $21$ after $\sim 4.8$ min, the ResNet at epoch $26$ after
    $\sim 5.3$ min). \textbf{Right}: event-level macro F1 on the
    synthetic test segment. The 1D-CNN and the CNN+Attention reach a
    perfect $1.00$; the ResNet sits at $0.92$ because of the single
    misclassification discussed above.}
    \label{fig:model-cost}
\end{figure}

On the synthetic test set the three architectures are clustered
within $8$ F1 points, with the smallest (1D-CNN) and the largest
(CNN+Attention) tied at the ceiling. The picture changes when we
move to robustness sweeps (\cref{chap:robustness}) and to the
real-data inference (\cref{chap:v2real}), where the differences in
the architectures' inductive biases re-emerge.
